Paper books, read aloud by the people you love.

Tantara turns the books already on your shelf into multi-voice audio stories. Each relative gets a color, records their own lines from their phone, and the child listens book in hand, with no screen to stare at.

See how it works

From paper page to family voices

No catalog, no cloned voices: your books, your family, your real voices.

1

Scan the pages

Photograph each page of a paper book and type the lines. The text only helps to split the story.

2

Color the voices

Give every character to a relative. One relative, one color. Add a tone per voice and a hint per sentence.

3

Record from the phone

Everyone opens the studio, sees their lines, and records them wherever they are.

4

Listen, book in hand

The child sees who is reading, hears a soft ding at each page end, and turns the paper page.

Made for children who cannot read yet

Everything a child touches is an icon, a face, a color or a sound.

Screenless listening

No text and no picture of the book is ever shown to the child, so there is no copyright worry and eyes stay on the paper pages.

Grimace photos

Every relative takes a funny, unique face. The child recognizes Grandpa in a blink.

Voice messages

Short voice notes between child and relatives. Listened once, the last five kept per conversation.

Colors everywhere

Six voice colors follow the story from the casting to the player, so the reader is always obvious.

Animated and playful

Icons, page transitions and small celebrations turn every tap into feedback.

Installable anywhere

A fast web app that installs on the home screen today, with Capacitor apps for the stores next.

Safe by design

Different roles, different powers, all enforced by the server.

Parents

Create books, validate what relatives propose, manage the family and reset codes. Protected by a numeric code.

Relatives

Propose books, record their own lines and leave voice messages. A parent approves every proposal.

Children

Listen and reply. They unlock their profile by tapping a short sequence of shapes, and a parent gets a mail code if it is ever forgotten.
